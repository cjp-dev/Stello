# Midgame search methods from Edax and Egaroucid worth testing in Stello

**Test the move hint after a failed ProbCut first.** Both Edax versions do this: when their shallow verification fails to cut, they look again for a move before ordering the full-depth search. Stello has already paid for that verification but can enter move ordering with the information it had *before* the search. This is a small experiment that neither changes the pruning threshold nor needs a new table.

I found two other ways Egaroucid avoids repeating work in deep searches, and one tactical difference that is reproducible in Stello. None is an established explanation for the depth gap. For scale, the [one-thread d20→22 effective branching factors](making-stello-6-0-findings.md#L527) were 1.678 for pre-Phase-4 Stello, 1.380 for Edax AVX and 1.546 for Egaroucid. The [landed hash-first change](making-stello-6-0-findings.md#L549) took about 2.7% off Stello's d22 nodes but left that factor at 1.679. A one-off saving and a slower-growing tree are both useful, but they are different results.

| Priority | Candidate | What it could improve |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recover the move from failed ProbCut verification (Edax and Edax AVX) | Earlier cutoffs at nodes that started without a hash move |
| 2 | Start the full re-search at the score the scout established (Egaroucid) | Less work when a later move challenges the principal variation |
| 3 | Narrow a wide window with a compatible bound on the current position (Egaroucid) | Less work on revisits; test as a consumer of Phase 4 item 2B |
| Tactical fix, separate from the depth ranking | Recognize an immediate wipeout before static leaf evaluation (Edax AVX; Egaroucid also checks at root and interior nodes) | Avoid overlooking a proven win at the horizon |

**Scope.** I compared active call sites in the [three local reference snapshots](making-stello-6-0-plan.md#L18) with Stello C++ at `2220202` (Phase 4 item 1A), the [Phase 4 plan](making-stello-6-0-plan.md#L284), recorded findings, and the [historical retest report](Deep%20midgame%20search%20experiments%20worth%20revisiting.md). I found no recorded Stello midgame A/B of the specific mechanisms below. That is not a claim that nobody has tried them. The only new measurements here are a small tactical reproduction and a board enumeration, not timings or matches.

## 1. Recover the move from an unsuccessful Multi-ProbCut verification

[Edax](../edax-reversi-master/src/midgame.c#L495) and [Edax AVX](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/midgame.c#L662) first try a transposition-table (TT) cutoff, then ProbCut. If search continues, there is more than one move, and the initial lookup gave no move, they read the ordinary TT again. The [verification searches the same board](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/midgame.c#L356) at reduced depth. It may have found a useful first move even though it could not justify pruning the node.

In Stello, [the initial TT lookup and handoff to deep ordering](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L724) straddle ProbCut, but the local `hash_move` is not refreshed. The [verification already returns a score **and a move**](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L1354); its caller retains only the cutoff decision. The [3.1 store-suppression trial](making-stello-3-1-findings.md#L292) showed that verification entries help *somewhere* in the tree. It did not test using the move immediately at its own parent search. Unlike internal iterative deepening, no extra search is needed.

**First experiment:** restrict this to deep null-window nodes with multiple moves, no initial legal hint, and a verification that actually ran and did not cut. Forward its legal move directly to [1A's existing hash-first path](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L1017); alternatively, test Edax's literal re-probe within the existing TT-read policy. Use the move **only for ordering**; never promote the shallow score or mark the move searched at full depth. Count qualifying nodes, legal hints, first-move cutoffs, sibling evaluations avoided, and time lost when the hint is wrong. Split failed high-side and low-side verifications: their returned moves need not be equally good. The strong cutoff rate measured for ordinary 1A hash moves is **not** an estimate for these weaker hints. Drop the idea if opportunities are rare or the new ordering is slower.

## 2. Use the scout's fail-soft score in the full re-search

A later move is normally scouted with a one-unit window to ask whether it beats the current best score, `alpha`. Sometimes the scout returns a stronger lower bound `g`, with `alpha < g < beta`. Egaroucid then searches the move over **`[g, beta]`** in parent coordinates, not `[alpha, beta]`. This is active in its [serial interior loop](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/midsearch.hpp#L312) and [serial root loop](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/midsearch.hpp#L607). [Edax AVX](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/midgame.c#L847) uses the **old alpha**: the faster reference engines do not agree on this choice.

Stello also uses old alpha at the [root](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L619), [shallow interior](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L827) and [deep interior](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L1116). Its [midgame split helper](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/ybwc_impl.hpp#L143) explicitly ignores the scout score, whereas its [endgame helper](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/ybwc_impl.hpp#L196) uses it. This is **not** root aspiration, coarser leaf scoring, or a proposal to omit the confirming search.

**First experiment:** log the distribution of `g - alpha` and time spent on those re-searches. A direct [Stello ProbCut](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L1360) returns the window edge, so not every scout success supplies a useful overshoot. Test serial interior nodes first, same horizon, MPC off, no cross-depth TT score reuse; check scores against the original-window control. With sound bounds, a confirming upper bound equal to `g` proves equality. A result **below** `g` contradicts the scout: retry with the original window without reusing the conflicting bound; do not clamp the score to `g`. Check move legality and optimality on ties. Only then test production MPC, log contradictions and changed moves, and check move quality. Selective scouts are not proofs. Reject if recovery and quality losses outweigh the saved re-search time.

## 3. Use a non-cutting TT bound to narrow a wide window on the same board

Egaroucid's [TT helper](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/transposition_cutoff.hpp#L19) does more than return exact hits and cutoffs. For a usable lower bound *inside* the window it raises alpha; for a usable upper bound it lowers beta. Its [interior PV caller](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/midsearch.hpp#L184) keeps the original window for [registration after search](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/midsearch.hpp#L338). Stello's [corresponding switch](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L727) takes immediate cutoffs but otherwise retains only the move. This is **not** enhanced transposition cutoff (ETC), which reads *child* positions. Nor does merely storing two bounds make search use them. One valid bound suffices for a one-sided trial.

**Test with item 2B, independently of candidate 2.** First count wide, non-root nodes where a compatible bound strictly improves an edge without cutting. A one-unit window has no interior integer edge to improve. Start with identical board, side, remaining horizon, evaluator, score domain and nonselective provenance; Stello's existing “stored depth at least requested depth” rule is not a proof that two heuristic horizons have the same value. Keep both the entry and working windows. If search fails at a tightened edge, combine only compatible bounds; an upper bound alone supplies no best-move witness. Do not store a narrowed-window failure as exact against the original window without the complementary proof. Test equality, conflicting bounds, tied moves and restricted roots before extending scope. Measure qualifying hits, saved re-search time and quality with production selectivity. Candidates 2 and 3 can exploit the **same** scout result via different routes; do not add their individual gains.

## A reproduced tactical difference: an immediate wipeout at the horizon

If a legal move flips every opposing disc, neither side can move again. [Edax AVX's one-ply evaluator](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/midgame.c#L185) recognizes this **before** evaluating patterns. Egaroucid checks for it while generating [root moves](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/midsearch.hpp#L563), [interior PV moves](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/midsearch.hpp#L227) and [null-window moves](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/midsearch_nws.hpp#L385). Stello can recognize the terminal on [entry to the child](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L701), but its [one-ply root](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L594) and ordinary [interior leaf evaluator](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/kernel.hpp#L121) can score the move as a pattern instead.

**Reproduction:** from the standard start play `f5 f6 d3 e3 f3 f4 f7 c5`. Black to move, 52 empties; Black `0x0020202038380000`, White `0x0000001c00000000` (`a1` = bit 0). Black has five legal moves; **b5 flips all three white discs**. I called the [native fixed-depth interface](../Stello.Cpp/src/ffi/stello_ffi.hpp#L96) with the built-in evaluator, one thread, MPC off and a cleared hash before each call:

| Root moves allowed | Depth 1: move, raw score | Depth 2: move, raw score |
|---|---|---|
| All | **e6, -862** | **b5, +32664** |
| b5 only | b5, -2612 | b5, +32664 |
| All except b5 | e6, -862 | e6, -850 |

This is a move-selection difference, not just a display convention. `+32664` is [Stello's terminal win score plus 64](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine.hpp#L540), **not** 64 heuristic centidiscs. The reproduction used the local Release benchmark DLL, SHA-256 `fb73372592accbb41aa74392b5eaf28d8394d9cfa12bc5bf31d0ac6054c0edb1`.

**Recommendation:** test `flips == opponent` where a legal move's flips are already in hand, before the leaf shortcut. Return Stello's terminal value, preserve the permitted root move set and the existing result-kind contract. No extra tree or root prepass. Test the position above, its color-swapped equivalent, exclusion of b5, and an ordinary pass with surviving opponent discs. This is different from the [rejected five-ply root clog search](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L394). It looks more important for tactical correctness than for depth: from the [24 deep benchmark roots](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Benchmark/Data/midgame-deep.txt), I enumerated all legal moves at the root and after one and two plies, counting paths rather than unique boards. There were **0 wipeouts in 265 + 3,184 + 38,119 = 41,568 legal moves**. That says nothing about incidence at d22 leaves, but it argues against this tactic being the main explanation for that panel.

## Two smaller ideas with narrower payoffs

- **Pass the current legal-move mask into MPC verification (Egaroucid): speed, not pruning.** Egaroucid sends the [unchanged board's legal mask into both verification branches](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/multi_probcut.hpp#L143); its [NWS regenerates it only when unknown](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/midsearch_nws.hpp#L356). Stello [already has the mask on entry](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L696), but omits it from the [same-board reduced search](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L1354). Forward it there. Separately, for the mobility evaluator, the [static MPC evaluation](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/kernel.hpp#L155) could use its popcount for the **moving side only**. Unlike caching a parent's ordering score, this also applies to an unscored hash move. Do not propagate it through a pass or to a different board. Require identical one-thread per-position moves, scores, depths, kinds **and nodes**; only wall time can justify it. It cannot reduce fixed-depth tree growth.
- **Reserve a small TT for auxiliary ordering searches (Edax AVX): defer.** Edax AVX's [expensive ordering key](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/move.c#L437) invokes shallow PVS at ordering depths 3–6. Its [sibling scouts and stores](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/midgame.c#L548) use a [separate table](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/search.c#L450); [ordinary shallow midgame NWS](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/midgame.c#L630) still uses the main table. Stello's [current deep ordering key](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L947) does not launch such searches. Do **not** take space from the main TT now, redirect ordinary shallow searches, or suppress MPC-root stores. Reconsider only if 3A adopts TT-writing auxiliary ordering searches and counters show those writes evict useful main-search entries. Compare shared vs partitioned at identical total bytes and include lost main-table hits.

## How to decide whether these explain deeper search

Start with candidate 1 at one thread. For 2 and 3, **measure the cost of the eligible nodes before coding both**; if re-searches or useful non-cutting hits account for little time, move on. Use cold identical roots, fixed TT bytes and separate switches. Compare paired per-root time and `sqrt(nodes[d+2]/nodes[d])` at d18→20 and d20→22, separating recursive visits from ordering evaluations. Confirm on the [36 same-empties roots from 12 other games](Deep%20midgame%20search%20experiments%20worth%20revisiting.md#a-check-against-overfitting-the-24-boards) before interpreting a slope change. Check ordinary completed depth and decisions at equal time, then the [existing move-quality and paired-match gates](making-stello-6-0-plan.md#L285). The reference code establishes that the methods are used; only those comparisons can show if they buy Stello another ply without buying worse moves.
