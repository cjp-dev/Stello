# Possible remaining Stello evaluation improvements

**Recommendation:** Keep the current evaluation as the control. Of the [Phase 6 proposals](making-stello-6-0-plan.md#L338), **better labels on positions Stello actually has to decide between** have the best chance of improving play without slowing search. Of the additional experiments below, I would train on **differences between legal alternatives**, not just on the score of the move played. Run the integer-rounding experiment as a cheap control, not as the leading explanation for Egaroucid's strength.

Two checks for this report support that order. Stello really does fit the historical scores better, even when both evaluators return whole discs. But when given the same positions and the same shallow search, neither evaluator chose demonstrably better moves. **We have evidence for a search-depth contribution, not evidence that Stello needs Egaroucid's post-training to catch up.**

## What the fit and the matches actually say

**The fit is real, but it measures a different task.** I ran the two shipped evaluation files on the same 1,040,276 positions selected by the [trainer's report](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Trainer/ReportCommand.cs#L39). This reproduces both the [5.0 Stello result](benchmark-results/eval-p5-e16-mob-s60-10m.json) and the [older Egaroucid comparison](benchmark-results/v2-p2-eval-p15-s10-m30-u8.json); the 5.0 report itself leaves the Egaroucid column blank.

| Output compared with the recorded side-to-move score | MAE (discs) | RMSE (discs) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Stello 5.0, raw sum / 16 (historical report) | 4.9691 | 6.7352 |
| Same weights, sum clipped to ±64 | 4.9468 | 6.7187 |
| Same weights, clipped and rounded to nearest whole disc | 4.9387 | 6.7240 |
| Egaroucid 7.9, its shipped whole-disc output | 5.0546 | 6.9014 |

The official MAE advantage is **0.0854 disc**. It is not an artifact of comparing fractions with integers. Only 5,123 predictions (0.49%) exceeded ±64; clipping those improves the offline numbers without changing a weight. [Stello's search already clamps its heuristic score](../Stello.Cpp/src/eval/eval.hpp#L11). The last two Stello rows are reporting controls, **not** weight post-training or new engine gains. Whole-disc rounding improves MAE here but worsens RMSE versus clipping alone.

Both engines use the [same 16 pattern shapes](../Stello.Net/Stello.Engine/Evaluation/Patterns.cs#L297), not the same function. Stello learns [two legal-mobility tables](../Stello.Net/Stello.Engine/Evaluation/EvalWeights.cs#L80); Egaroucid adds [a side-to-move disc-count table](../Stello.Net/Egaroucid.Engine/Evaluate.cs#L133). The main corpus labels a position by the outcome of **one continuation** after a random opening, largely level-11 Egaroucid versus Edax. The smaller position source also includes searched early labels; [the converter documents both sources](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Trainer/ConvertCommand.cs#L11). These labels are not the minimax values of all legal alternatives. Also, 400,000 test positions (38.5%) have only 1–20 empties, where a timed engine can often search to the end rather than use a static value as its answer. The very earliest 56–60-empty bucket has just four positions. This is a useful regression set, not a sample weighted by decisions that settle matches.

**Stello does beat native Egaroucid in some conditions.** These are the [book-off Phase 0 matches](making-stello-6-0-findings.md#L166), 1,174 games per cell, against the native C++ opponent, not the slower managed port. The weights did not change between rows.

| Time / threads | Stello 5.0 score (95% interval) | Reported midgame plies, Stello / Egaroucid |
|---|---:|---:|
| 500 ms / 1 | **55.0%** (52.7–57.3) | 20.37 / 19.07 |
| 2 s / 1 | **42.2%** (40.1–44.2) | 23.00 / 25.90 |
| 500 ms / 8 | **44.8%** (42.8–46.8) | 23.77 / 26.58 |
| 2 s / 8 | **48.1%** (46.2–50.0) | 26.63 / 29.19 |

The crossover makes effective search depth, selectivity and parallel scaling more credible explanations than a uniformly inferior evaluation. It does **not** establish how many Elo each contributes. At 2 s/1t the [first-solve averages](benchmark-results/match_p0_v5_vs_egaroucid_cpp_2000ms_1t.md) were 25.0 / 24.8 empties despite the 2.9-ply midgame difference. [The depth counters](Possible%20remaining%20Stello%20search%20improvments.md#L19) include selective terminal passes and treat interrupted iterations differently. Conversely, evaluation genuinely matters: [adding mobility to Stello](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L476) improved fit and scored 52.9% against the previous model at 2 s, despite a lower node rate. That was a within-Stello gain, not a head-to-head evaluator swap with Egaroucid.

### A controlled move-choice check

I used all [300 roots with exact scores for every legal move](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Benchmark/Data/midgame-exact.txt): 100 Stello self-play games, sampled around 28, 26 and 24 empties; 3,037 legal root alternatives. Both shipped evaluators got the **identical full-width minimax tree**, at 1, 2 or 3 placements: no book, clock, pruning, TT or special solver. Forced passes cost no placement; double passes get the terminal disc margin. Stello used its deployed centidisc conversion and clamp, Egaroucid its whole-disc output. No weights were fitted to this suite.

| Plies | Root-move value MAE, Stello / Egaroucid | Exact discs lost by chosen move, Stello / Egaroucid | Paired loss difference, Stello minus Egaroucid (95% interval) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 6.960 / 7.217 | 3.113 / 3.062 | +0.051 [−0.316, +0.460] |
| 2 | 5.114 / 5.313 | 2.337 / 2.376 | −0.039 [−0.317, +0.242] |
| 3 | 5.154 / 5.266 | 1.937 / 1.999 | −0.062 [−0.329, +0.209] |

For reproducibility: average absolute move-value error within each root, then equally across roots. Disc loss is `best exact margin − exact margin of the selected move`; average uniformly over tied predicted best moves, **not** over the oracle's best-first file order. Intervals resample the 100 source games as blocks (10,000 paired bootstrap draws, seed 611). At one ply the evaluators picked an optimal move about 55% of the time each; at three plies, 64.5% / 63.4%. **Stello had lower value error at every depth; move loss was indistinguishable at each.** Adding two plies helped both much more than swapping evaluators here. This narrow, previously published late-midgame suite cannot establish equality at match depth or stand in for a fresh holdout.

## Is Egaroucid's post-training the missing step?

It is a real algorithm, but there is no matched before/after game result here to attribute the gap to it. The [CUDA optimizer](../Egaroucid-main/src/tools/evaluation/eval_optimizer_cuda.cu#L655) starts with nearest-integer weights. It tries flipping one coefficient (and its reflected partner) between floor and ceiling, accepts non-worsening **training MSE**, and applies a time limit. Its [loss function](../Egaroucid-main/src/tools/evaluation/eval_optimizer_cuda.cu#L291) rounds the **sum** to whole discs and clips it, just as Egaroucid will use it. The [after-training wrapper](../Egaroucid-main/src/tools/evaluation/after_training_wrapper.py) is export/reporting, not a second round of self-play. The [native match runner](../Stello.Net/tools/Egaroucid.Runner/runner.cpp#L133) loads finished weights; the accuracy comparator also uses finished weights. Neither compares an untreated Egaroucid with a treated one.

**How to test it in Stello:** freeze the *same* post-pruning floating checkpoint; export nearest 1/16 as control, then coordinate-optimized 1/16. Optimize Stello's own clamped, sub-disc output, preserve folded symmetries and the rare-entry policy, and report float → nearest → optimized on untouched positions, including sibling ties and regret. Fit only on training data; select the stopping point on validation. **Residual clipping and appearance-based Adam are separate training experiments**, not part of rounding and not fixes for quantization. The older [1/16 vs 1/8 Stello trial](improve-stello-strength-findings.md#L92) barely changed fit on a different model; it argues for measuring the current loss first, not for assuming it is zero. A small free gain is plausible. A large match-strength gain is unsupported.

## Which current Phase 6 ideas would I prioritize?

These are judgments about the chance of improving **play at equal time**, not probabilities or promised Elo. Lower static error, cheaper delivery and stronger play are three different outcomes.

| Phase 6 item | Assessment and suggested change |
|---|---|
| **A: fresh self-play, deep relabels, WTHOR/GGS, opening coverage** | **Best bet for strength.** First compare old vs new labels on the *same* boards at equal sample count, then change the board distribution. Include alternatives rejected by search, balanced positions and forced passes. Use human games for positions, not their final score as perfect play. Distinguish exact labels from completed deep estimates and bounds; cross-check a sample with a different teacher. Split by game/opening family before augmentation; reserve an independent test that is never used for training, feature choice or pruning calibration. |
| **A: richer shapes; stability/parity** | **Medium** if a cheap feature fixes a measured residual. Inventory the [existing shapes](../Stello.Net/Stello.Engine/Evaluation/Patterns.cs#L297): corner 3×3 and edge+2X are not new. Try replacing one expensive/redundant shape before adding another. The [5.0 ablation](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L479) already tried potential mobility, quadrant parity and disc difference alongside mobility; mobility captured about 85–90% of the combined gain on its three stages. An exact stability computation at every leaf has a higher bar than a cheap edge feature. |
| **A: integer rounding, clipping, appearance-capped Adam** | **Low cost to test; modest expected upside.** For clipping, count saturation and error in close positions as well as blowouts. The upstream [count cap](../Egaroucid-main/src/tools/evaluation/eval_optimizer_cuda.cu#L549) scales the step **outside** [Adam's normalization](../Egaroucid-main/src/tools/evaluation/eval_optimizer_cuda.cu#L388); [Stello](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Trainer/TrainCommand.cs#L372) normalizes the gradient **before** its moments and has a rare-weight prior. Merely changing the denominator there is not the same experiment. Monitor frequent/rare buckets, separately from export. |
| **B1: pattern-embedding NNUE** | **Highest upside for accuracy; uncertain timed strength.** Start with a small, phase-shared trunk and a linear-pattern control trained on identical labels. Even 64 `int16` values per 320,193 folded indices are about **39 MiB** before other layers or unfolding. Measure accumulator updates for flips, changes of side and passes, and sibling ordering, not just an isolated dense-layer benchmark. The proposed 4.3–4.5 MAE is an unverified target. |
| **B2: compact square-input NNUE** | **Best packaging bet, weaker first desktop-strength bet.** [Yamana's own Othello experiment](https://www.egaroucid.nyanyan.dev/en/technology/explanation/) found a small square-input net less accurate than patterns on *his* data; that is not a Stello forecast. Compare at equal transfer size **and** at equal browser move time. Use the existing 8.96 MB Brotli model as control; a 1–3 MB download alone is not a strength pass. |
| **B3: small/big dual evaluator** | **Last, conditional.** First establish that the big model picks better moves often enough and measure disagreement by phase and distance from *both* window edges. Use the cheap model just for ordering in the first trial. “Far from beta” is not proof of a safe cutoff; validate false cuts on wide-window references, keep heuristic bounds out of the exact solver, and keep transposition scores compatible across evaluation paths. |
| **Refit MPC/MPC-end; desktop/web decision** | **Necessary controls, not a standalone evaluation gain.** Compare evaluators at fixed depth with selective pruning off, then with a separate margin fit for each and finally on the clock. Check false cuts in the tails, not just overall RMSE. Both [midgame](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Trainer/MpcCommand.cs#L45) and [endgame](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Trainer/MpcCommand.cs#L126) calibration currently draw from `Split.Test`; reserve a disjoint calibration set for Phase 6. Split web/desktop only if native **and** browser results justify two models and their pruning fits. |

## Additional experiments not found in the Stello trials

I checked the [earlier model grid and matches](improve-stello-strength-findings.md#L55), [5.0 trials](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L452), Phase 6, the trainer and commit history. “Additional” means no matching Stello trial found, not that the method is new to Othello.

1. **Train the value difference between sibling moves.** For a sample of real search roots, label the best few candidates **and a sampled alternative** with exact margins or independent deep searches. Add a loss on `(predicted A − predicted B) − (teacher A − teacher B)` to the ordinary disc-value loss, with both scores expressed for the parent. Do not force an order for teacher ties or differences smaller than the teacher's uncertainty. Retain absolute calibration for pruning. Compare **same positions, labels, weights format and update budget**, scalar-only versus scalar-plus-difference; keep complete sibling groups in one split. The controlled test above explains why this is the first new experiment: better marginal predictions did not consistently buy better choices. No extra inference cost.
2. **Learn joint mobility rather than two independent counts.** Today the mobility contribution is `own[a] + opponent[b]`; it cannot express “one extra move is especially valuable when *both* sides have few moves.” Both counts are already computed. Fit a small, regularized two-dimensional residual over coarse count bins on top of the current marginals. Check held-out residuals and regret specifically in the low-mobility cells before a match. It can be expanded at load time into a single 48×48 table per stage (276,480 bytes total at `int16` across 60 stages, **if** the combined entries fit; otherwise widen). This separates testing an interaction from adding more move generation or a full NNUE.
3. **Give rare pattern weights a same-parity prior rather than always zero.** [The trainer](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Trainer/TrainCommand.cs#L408) drops entries below an appearance threshold and [copies the nearest trained stage](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Trainer/TrainCommand.cs#L80) where a stage is missing. Fit a shared estimate for the same folded index at disc counts `d−2, d, d+2`; let high-count stages depart from it and shrink sparse ones toward it. This is **not** the [rejected adjacent-disc smoothing](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L455), which mixed opposite parities. Export ordinary tables; compare rare versus common entries, odd versus even stages, book-off early moves, and compressed bytes. Stop if it only makes a larger file or helps boards never encountered by search.
4. **Train small per-pattern networks, but ship lookup tables, not an in-search network.** The [paper cited by Track B](https://doi.org/10.1109/TG.2025.3624825) and [Yamana's explanation](https://www.egaroucid.nyanyan.dev/en/technology/explanation/) describe learning a local function and enumerating its finite inputs. Train a shared small network for each shape from its ternary squares and stage; enforce the same symmetries, enumerate the entries offline, then export to Stello's existing format. Compare to direct table training on identical data, especially on rare configurations. This shares information among related configurations **without** matrix operations at a leaf. It cannot represent cross-pattern interactions, and exporting full tables does not automatically shrink the download. Shipping a compact generator and expanding at startup is a *separate* web experiment with an initialization and RAM cost.

**Decision order:** (1) Freeze evaluation, decision and MPC-calibration splits; keep the existing suite as a diagnostic, not another tuning set. (2) Try stronger labels and the sibling objective against the present linear model; run the rounding control separately. (3) Try joint mobility and parity-aware shrinkage before adding inference cost. (4) Compare the offline table generator and the smallest credible NNUE on identical data. Promote a candidate only after fixed-depth move regret, equal-time search and the [paired Phase 6 gates](making-stello-6-0-plan.md#L354). No production weights were retrained or changed, and no timed matches were run for this report.
