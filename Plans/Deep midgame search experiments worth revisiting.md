# Which Stello search experiments are worth repeating at greater depth?

**Verdict (27 November 2026): yes, selectively.** The new baseline shows a one-thread search cost that grows faster with depth than either opponent's. Shallow tests could have hidden improvements that repay their cost only when they prevent a large subtree search. They do **not** establish that Stello's old tuning caused the gap. My first two retests would be **move ordering after the hash move fails** and **the cost and accuracy of deep Multi-ProbCut verification**. I would not start by changing the thread count, buying a larger hash table, or replaying all the old matches.

This assessment uses the [pre-Phase 4 baseline](making-stello-6-0-findings.md#L522), the [landed Phase 4 item 1A](making-stello-6-0-findings.md#L537), and the historical results below. All new calculations are from saved results, benchmark boards or the current formula; no engine benchmark or match was run for this report.

## What the deeper benchmark actually shows

Same 24 positions and one thread, depth 16 to 22. The first two columns are ratios **within** each engine; the last column is elapsed time for all 24 roots, not time per move.

| Engine | Node growth, d22 / d16 | Time growth, d22 / d16 | d16 → d22 wall time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Stello, pre-Phase 4 | 20.57× | 19.10× | 1.964 → 37.52 s |
| Edax AVX | 6.89× | 6.31× | 0.768 → 4.848 s |
| Egaroucid, MPC 74 | 11.08× | 11.66× | 2.053 → 23.94 s |

Stello goes from slightly faster than Egaroucid to **1.57× its time**; the Edax time gap widens from **2.56× to 7.74×**. Stello's one-thread throughput actually rises from 11.98M to 12.89M nodes/s. This is not a sudden slowdown at d22: its observed effective branching factor (EBF, `sqrt(nodes[d+2] / nodes[d])`) stays near **1.64–1.68**. A persistent difference compounds. Nor is it primarily a thread-scaling diagnosis: at d22 Stello gets **4.92×** speedup from eight threads, versus **2.68×** for Edax and **4.37×** for Egaroucid. The gap is already present on one.

**Item 1A is a useful control.** Searching the hash move before scoring siblings reduced Stello's one-thread nodes by **2.81% at d16 and 2.68% at d22**. From the [exact counts](making-stello-6-0-findings.md#L549), d20→22 EBF was **1.678 before, 1.679 after**. Keep the speed saving; do not mistake it for a change in growth rate. Conversely, don't infer that the opponents prune *correctly* more often from these totals: node definitions, selectivity, iterative-deepening schedules and hash budgets differ. The single-pass d22 timings also give no estimate of run-to-run variation. Pure midgame means no root endgame handover, **not** full-width or equal-quality search.

**Not all the old evidence was shallow.** The 5.0 aspiration test reached [fixed d18](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L363); ProbCut, a second hash move and hash capacity had [two-second matches](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L308). Pre-Phase 4 Stello's new d22 run averaged **1.56 s/root at 1t and 0.32 s/root at 8t**. Two seconds was not intrinsically too short. What was missing was the *same early positions*, a range of *completed ordinary depths*, and measurements of *where the extra work went*. A match's [average heuristic depth](../Stello.Net/Stello.Engine/BookMatch.cs#L22) is not that experiment.

## Which old decisions to reopen

### 1. First: how much to spend ordering the moves that remain after a hash miss or failed hash move

The [original test](improve-stello-strength-findings.md#L352) was unusually informative about its limit: d12, 100 roots, and an engine reaching about 17 plies in play. Searching **every child** to a quarter of the remaining depth, even when enabled only from look 8, took **4.55 s versus 3.26 s** for the old-ordering control. The cheaper evaluation-ordering and ETC combination took **3.24 s**. This is good evidence against the blanket quarter-depth rule. It is poor evidence against spending **one or two plies on a handful of candidates** when a wrong first move will cause a d18+ full-window re-search. Egaroucid itself [uses different costs for wide and null windows](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/move_ordering.hpp#L482); its special selective ordering rule from remaining depth 25 cannot explain the observed d22 gap.

**Test:** keep 1A and its fast hash cutoff. On the remaining deep principal-variation (PV) nodes, first try a one-ply ordering search only on the top few candidates from the existing key. Leave null-window nodes unchanged as a control. Record ordering milliseconds, first-choice success and **full-window re-search milliseconds saved**, by remaining depth. Count time in shortlisted and non-shortlisted moves; abandon the trial if it only moves work into ordering. This is a narrower version of [Phase 4 item 3A](making-stello-6-0-plan.md#L302), not a second implementation of 1A.

**Cheaper alternative, worth testing separately:** the [current key](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L988) still uses 35/17/269 for reply mobility, potential mobility and evaluation, as in the old test. The [new evaluator itself includes mobility](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L478). That does **not** prove mobility is counted wrongly; the best cutoff predictor need not be the best evaluator. But it gives a reason to retest the old weights without adding any lookahead: on held-out games, does the same key still rank the eventual cutoff or PV move first after a hash miss? Only time an alternative key if its ordering improves there.

### 2. Measure deep ProbCut verification before another global margin sweep

The [original MPC labels](improve-stello-strength-findings.md#L305) stopped at d13. The [5.0 error-model comparison](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L296) fitted depths 0–13; the alternative `powergap` model scored **50.1% at 2 s**, not a hidden long-time win. Those experiments preceded the production mobility evaluator. The [later evaluator fits](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L460) used 3,900 positions each; the non-mobility fit explicitly reports d0–12. I found no reported validation of the production model's false-cut rate on live high-depth cut nodes. That is the reason to reopen this, **not** an assumption that a smaller margin is better.

**A concrete lead:** I evaluated the [production mobility-model formula](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine.cpp#L31) at default `t=1` for all 970 `(discs, remaining depth)` pairs with `4 <= discs`, `3 <= depth <= 22`, and `discs + depth <= 64`. The computed verification margin spans **0.50–3.61 discs**. The [static eligibility filter](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L1294) is `max(1 disc, margin - 4 discs)`: it is therefore **exactly one disc in all 970 cells**. This is a calculation of the threshold, not a measurement of how often it fires. The separate verification margin still varies. At remaining d20 and d22, for example, the engine pays for a **d10** verification in both.

**Test:** on the current engine, log allowed/rejected static probes, verification time, cuts and failed probes by remaining depth, node type, distance from the window and parity. Sample **both** accepted and rejected probes; check a bounded, held-out sample against deeper nonselective results without reusing selective TT bounds. If the near-window bucket wastes most of its time verifying, test a *tighter* eligibility filter there; if it screens out profitable cuts, try the *more permissive* filter in [item 4B](making-stello-6-0-plan.md#L307). Test one change at a time, before recursion or changing probe depth. If most cost is actually in remaining d4–10, target those nodes even though the **root** is d22. Do not commission an exhaustive d22 nonselective training run as the first step.

### 3. Conditional retests and experiments to leave closed

| Earlier experiment | Decision for deep search |
|---|---|
| **ETC and TT replacement** — [2.0 ETC](improve-stello-strength-findings.md#L352) was swept at d12; [3.1 retention of a tighter, shallower bound](making-stello-3-1-findings.md#L217) saved 1.94% of d10 nodes but did not establish a match gain at 500 ms or 2 s. | **Revisit at fixed bytes** in items 2A/2B/4A. By depth, count useful child bounds, avoided subtree work and eviction of still-useful deep entries, not just hit rate. Test warm consecutive turns separately: [the benchmark clears the table for each root](../Stello.Cpp/src/cli/bench.cpp#L356). Do not merge an incompatible shallow bound into a deep proof. |
| **Second TT move** — [5.0](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L329) found no midgame depth gain; the combined mid/end variant scored **48.7% at 2 s**. | **Conditional, not a full revival.** Item 2E now has a changed premise: after 1A, a second immediate cutoff can avoid scoring the siblings altogether. Measure how often a distinct, legal second move would cut **after the first fails** and how much ordering it would avoid. Keep the endgame out of that A/B. |
| **Internal iterative deepening (IID)** — [the old no-hash test](improve-stello-strength-findings.md#L357) at d12 was essentially flat; iterative deepening and MPC already supplied moves in nearly every deep node. | **Count opportunities first.** Measure the fraction **and time share** of deep, no-usable-hash PV nodes under the intended TT replacement. Only if expensive misses survive, retry short IID there. IID spends a shallow search to *find a move*; Phase 4's IIR *reduces the main search depth*. They are not interchangeable. |
| **Aspiration** — [5.0](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L363) already found ±1 disc added 4.04% d16 nodes; wider windows saved little through d18. | **Do not rerun the old fixed widths first.** Test +2-ply iteration with full windows; only then try a same-parity prediction with dual bounds. Count fail-high **and** fail-low retry time, and the last completed fallback at timeout. The saved same-parity scores below justify measuring this, not enabling it. |
| **Lazy SMP / split thresholds** — [Phase 3](making-stello-6-0-findings.md#L449) tuned the midgame helper mix largely on d14/d16. | **Secondary, 8t only.** Repeat the current mix against one neighboring helper count or split threshold after the single-thread tree stabilizes. d22 still has **39.1%** extra nodes at 8t, but threads cannot explain the measured one-thread slope. |
| **Bigger hash, root clog, fixed soft time limit** — [64→256 MB](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L381) already lost 9.3% NPS and 0.39 reported ply at 2 s/8t; the [five-ply clog prepass](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L394) scored 49.2% at 500 ms; [flat soft limits](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L348) mainly bought more time. | **Leave closed for this question.** Bigger RAM is not a substitute for a better replacement policy. A root-only prepass might amortize, but needs a board class with actual hits before retesting. A soft clock cannot cause a fixed-depth slope. Likewise, 1A's [lazy selection and shallow hash-first variants](making-stello-6-0-findings.md#L540) have **already** been rejected on deep comparisons. |

## A check against overfitting the 24 boards

I joined the [new panel](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Benchmark/Data/midgame-deep.txt) to the archived one-thread [5.0 d14](benchmark-results/v5.0-ref/midgame-depth14.json) and [d16](benchmark-results/v5.0-ref/midgame-depth16.json) results by position ID, requiring the requested depth and heuristic kind. All 24 boards match the [parent corpus](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Benchmark/Data/midgame.txt); they are **three snapshots from each of eight games**, not 24 independent games. Another 12 games supply 36 disjoint roots at exactly the same empties.

| Saved Stello 5.0 results | d14 nodes | d16 nodes | d14→16 EBF | Root move changed | Absolute score change >2 discs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Phase 4 panel, 24 roots | 7,812,353 | 23,516,883 | **1.735** | 3/24 | 1/24 |
| Other 12 games, 36 roots | 9,108,295 | 23,519,501 | **1.607** | 3/36 | 1/36 |

The panel's archived d16 total even matches the exact pre-Phase 4 figure. The slope difference does **not** invalidate the head-to-head result: the three engines faced the same roots. It does show why a change tuned on those eight games needs confirmation on other games. The score changes are full-window, two-ply differences, **not** measured aspiration failures or true move quality. All panel positions have Black to move and even empties; add odd-empty/White-to-move roots for parity-sensitive tests rather than treating another pass over the same boards as independent evidence.

## Suggested sequence for the Phase 4 agent

1. **Pin the control to 1A (`2220202`) or a named later commit.** Capture per-root and per-iteration work at d16/18/20/22, one thread, same binary settings and total hash bytes. Split ordering-evaluation nodes from recursive search visits; record remaining depth and PV/null windows. Count work in instrumented builds, but time clean builds. To distinguish ordering from selective pruning, repeat a promising ordering A/B on a small, affordable one-thread subset with MPC off in **both** variants. No need to repeat the external benchmark for each variant.
2. **Run one candidate at a time.** Compare paired wall time and node growth at d18→20 and d20→22; show per-game results as well as suite totals. Interleaved A/B/B/A at all four one-thread depths would cost roughly **four minutes of search per candidate** at baseline speeds, excluding builds, instrumentation and any holdout. Advance a candidate that helps at deep depths without unacceptable shallow cost; confirm on the other 12 games and at 8t. A threshold that actually starts at remaining d25 needs a separate d26+ pilot on early boards with verified midgame mode, not a null result at d22.
3. **Keep speed, depth and strength separate.** Compare decisions at equal time, use exact disc loss where labels exist and label early-board deeper references as *references*, not ground truth. Preserve the [paired 500 ms / 2 s strength gate](making-stello-6-0-plan.md#L316). If a candidate only wins at a genuinely longer control, describe it as a long-control candidate and agree on that separate gate before running matches; don't silently change the release rule.
