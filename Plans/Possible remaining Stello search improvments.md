# Possible remaining Stello search improvments

**Recommendation:** Try the legal transposition-table (TT) move **before scoring its siblings**. This is the best Phase 4 bet for completing a deeper search without deliberately searching some alternatives less deeply. The best additional bet is to **start a new turn from a verified previous principal variation** (PV, the engine's current best line), instead of rebuilding root ordering from depth one. I would test these separately. Neither requires waiting for the entire unified TT redesign.

I expect Phase 4 to buy some completed depth, but **would not forecast that it closes Edax's five-to-six reported plies**. The measurements below explain why. This is an investigation of the [26 November snapshot](../Content%20and%20state%20of%20the%20Stello%20project%20as%20of%202026.11.26.md), not a new engine or match result. The three additional experiments below are ones for which I found no Stello A/B in the plans, findings or commit subjects. They are separate from the [unchanged-tree speed proposals](Possible%20remaining%20Stello%20speed%20optimizations.md).

## How big is the gap?

[Phase 0 paired matches](making-stello-6-0-findings.md#L166), book off, 1,174 games each. Each cell is **opponent / Stello 5.0** average reported midgame plies. These are not post-Phase-2 external matches.

| Native opponent | 500 ms, 1 thread | 500 ms, 8 threads | 2 s, 1 thread | 2 s, 8 threads |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Edax | 25.87 / 20.39 | 29.49 / 23.76 | 29.39 / 23.04 | 32.80 / 26.86 |
| Edax AVX | 26.05 / 20.39 | 29.65 / 23.79 | 29.60 / 23.17 | 32.90 / 26.87 |
| Egaroucid | **19.07 / 20.37** | 26.58 / 23.77 | 25.90 / 23.00 | 29.19 / 26.63 |

- **Edax's lead is real as a reported statistic; extra thinking time is not a good explanation.** At [500 ms, one thread](benchmark-results/match_p0_v5_vs_edax_avx_500ms_1t.md), Edax AVX averaged 249 ms and Stello 256 ms per searched move. Edax reported 43.65M vs 20.38M nodes/s, but the engines do not count the same work. Its first solve was only about **one empty** earlier (23.9 vs 22.9), not six. Egaroucid actually searched shallower and lost at 500 ms/1t; at [500 ms/8t](benchmark-results/match_p0_v5_vs_egaroucid_cpp_500ms_8t.md) it reported greater depth **despite lower NPS**, with first solve 25.4 for both. Separate single-thread search, parallel efficiency and exact solving.
- **A ply is expensive.** On the 40 identical Stello 5.0 benchmark roots with at least 40 empties and completed heuristic depths [14](benchmark-results/v5.0-ref/midgame-depth14.json) and [16](benchmark-results/v5.0-ref/midgame-depth16.json), summed nodes rose from 12,087,428 to 32,837,114. That is about **1.65 times the work per ply**. On *that* tree 10% more work per second buys about 0.19 ply, not a whole ply. This is a scale for expectations, not a conversion from Edax nodes to Stello plies. The actual [Phase 2 vs 5.0 match](benchmark-results/match_p2_v6_vs_v5_100ms_1t.json) gained 9.75% reported NPS at 100 ms but just **17.36 vs 17.29** reported plies.

### A reporting difference worth measuring before changing search

The match [counts every heuristic result](../Stello.Net/Stello.Engine/BookMatch.cs#L22) and [divides by that side's number of heuristic moves](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Benchmark/MatchReport.cs#L22). The engines need not be on the same boards, or even count the same game phases. First solve is a separate average over games in which that side solved. Three different things can look like an extra ply:

| What happened | What the depth means |
|---|---|
| An ordinary root iteration ran out of time | Stello [commits a new best inside the root move loop](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L625); its [iteration-done signal](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L477) is later. A nominal depth can represent only part of the root. Egaroucid's [timed driver](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/ai.hpp#L342) publishes the new depth on a successful iteration. The [Edax adapter](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Benchmark/EdaxMatchPlayer.cs#L175) takes the last observer line before the move, not a certificate that all root moves finished at that depth. |
| The engine switched to a selective end search | At **32 empties**, Stello's [ordinary loop](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L472) goes through depth **24** before attempting [selective terminal passes](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L198). Egaroucid's [timed schedule](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/ai.hpp#L439) can go from **20 straight to 32 at its 74% setting**. Completing that pass earns a displayed 32 without completing ordinary depths 21 through 31. Both adapters count a selective terminal result as heuristic: [Stello](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L205), [Egaroucid](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Benchmark/EgaroucidCppMatchPlayer.cs#L165). [Edax](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/root.c#L775) also switches from iterating depth to iterating selectivity. These settings are not comparable probabilities of choosing the right move. |
| A reduction or extension changed only some lines | A completed depth 22 with late moves reduced to 21 is not 22 plies of equal coverage. Conversely, extending the PV to a proof can improve the move while the last completed ordinary depth **falls**. Track both instead of optimizing the display. |

Do not change Stello's handover just to imitate the second row. [Earlier handovers were actually tested](improve-stello-strength-findings.md#L413); moving four squares earlier with the then-selective solver scored 47.9%. The later [5.0 staged endgame](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L432) is different, and Phase 5 already owns the solve-mode work.

## Which Phase 4 items are most likely to help?

These are qualitative chances of improving **last completed ordinary root depth at equal wall time**, not Elo predictions. “High” means there is identifiable work to remove without sacrificing horizon; “mixed” means either direction is plausible. The [Phase 4 list](making-stello-6-0-plan.md#L276) groups several changes that should not share an A/B switch.

| Phase 4 item | Depth outlook | How I would change the experiment |
|---|---|---|
| **2. Hash move first; lazy selection** | **Highest confidence.** On a first-move cutoff the remaining scores are unused. | The [current deep path](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L872) already caches flips/replies and rank-sorts, but evaluates every non-hash sibling before playing the hash move. Keep ETC placement, the first move and remaining tie order unchanged initially. Count eligible nodes, first-move cutoffs and sibling evaluations avoided. Don't assume the plan's 80–90% holds for Stello. Then try selecting only the next needed move. A lower NPS can still mean a faster search because ordering evaluations count as nodes. |
| **2. Dedicated depth-1/2 null-window search (NWS); deeper node-type ordering** | **Good** for cheaper frequent NWS leaves; **mixed** for spending more on ordering. | Preserve pass handling, the forced-move horizon extension and bound semantics in the shallow kernels. For deep ordering, try a cheap key on cut nodes first; spend a one- or two-ply search on PV candidates only where it reduces full-window re-searches. Test stale-hash guard and child-TT/stability bonuses separately. [Quarter-depth searches on every child already cost more than they saved](improve-stello-strength-findings.md#L354). |
| **1. Unified TT, two bounds, replacement, protected PV table** | **Medium** for ordinary midgame, more promising across the endgame transition. Can go **backwards**. | Keep **total bytes** fixed, including PV storage. Compare unification, associativity/replacement, dual bounds, then PV protection. Measure usable cutoffs rather than raw hits; isolate forced/leaf write suppression and parallel contention. Never confuse replacement priority with proof validity: distinguish heuristic units, WLD and exact margin; only merge compatible depth/selectivity bounds. “No ProbCut at this depth” is not a terminal proof. Defer the second move: [5.0 got no depth gain](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L331). A [64-to-256 MiB test](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L383) **lost 0.39 ply and 9.3% NPS** at 2 s/8t. [Suppressing shallow ProbCut-root stores](making-stello-3-1-findings.md#L291) cost 4.7–6.2% more nodes; don't discard those hints indiscriminately. |
| **3. Enhanced transposition/stability cutoffs (ETC/ESC)** | **Medium** when valid child bounds avoid whole subtrees; **lower** far from the end or when most probes miss. | First measure saved children per scan by depth and window. Try recording a valid parent cutoff and skipping already refuted children before adding speculative probes. The current [ETC loop](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L872) sits inside the deep-ordering path and checks hash height; changing the named ETC minimum alone won't change that loop. Physical stability bounds prove final disc limits, not arbitrary midgame evaluator values: keep the score domains explicit. |
| **3. Recursive/non-PV ProbCut, prefilter, static depth-3 cuts, error refit** | **Medium to high for nominal depth** from cheaper verification; **mixed for useful depth**. Non-PV-only **alone tends shallower**, since it removes existing pruning on PV nodes. | Test recursion capped at one extra level, PV exclusion, and cheaper static cuts independently. Keep the current evaluator's error model as control; sample rejected branches with deeper nonselective verification and count false cuts. A [5.0 margin experiment](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L311) got **19.39 vs 19.14 plies** yet scored **49.5%**. More aggressive pruning is not the same as better search. |
| **3. Two-ply iterative deepening; aspiration windows** | **Medium** for the stride; **low by itself** for narrow aspiration. Either can leave a shallower completed fallback at timeout. | Try **+2 with a full root window first**, retaining a quick fallback and honoring requested fixed depth. Measure work saved in skipped iterations against extra work in the next one. Only then size aspiration windows from same-parity score changes; count fail-high/low retries and their time. [Previous narrow aspiration](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L365) added up to **4.04%** depth-16 nodes; wider windows bought essentially no timed depth. |
| **3. PV extension into exact endgame** | **Usually neutral or negative for ordinary root depth; positive for selected line coverage** if the proof is affordable. | Start near the solve boundary and cap extension cost. Judge by proven outcomes, exact disc loss and first solve, not by average ordinary plies. Keep an extended PV distinct from a proven root result. |
| **4. Time management and verified early exit** | **Deeper on unstable moves; intentionally shallower on easy ones.** Not a general speedup. | Test per-game clocks separately from per-move limits, where saved time cannot be transferred. Egaroucid's [early exit](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/ai.hpp#L359) requires stability across iterations, depth 29–30, at least 44 empties and a separate search of alternatives. Do not stop on an unchanged move alone. [Flat soft limits](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L350) bought 0.11–0.15 ply without passing; [avoiding predicted unfinished iterations](improve-stello-strength-findings.md#L263) lost strength. Keep useful partial results. |
| **4. Internal iterative reductions (IIR); late-move reductions (LMR) with continuation/countermove history** | **Likely deeper on paper when reductions fire; coverage becomes shallower.** IIR may barely fire: [earlier Stello often had a deep-node hash move](improve-stello-strength-findings.md#L385). | Try history **for ordering alone** first. Then reduce one ply on late non-PV cut-node moves, re-search improvements at full depth, and audit a sample of reduced fail-lows (the dangerous misses). Try no-hash IIR on cut nodes before PV nodes. Track best-move errors, especially corners, forced replies and mobility swings. |
| **4. Correction history; root subtree-cost ordering** | **Low** direct depth chance for correction history; **modest** for root ordering, with less risk. | Try previous subtree cost as a tie-break among non-PV root moves, retaining the incumbent first; measure time to refute challengers. For correction history, first establish held-out residual bias, then use it in the ProbCut prefilter **before** changing leaf scores. Don't learn an “exact” correction from a failed scout or a selective bound. |

## Three additional experiments

### 1. Reuse the previous turn's search at the new root

[Stello](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L124) clears response ordering, orders the root with no hash move and [starts at `look = 0`](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L471), even when the opponent followed a searched line. Descendant TT entries can still be useful; this is **not** a proposal to keep the entire TT alive longer. [Edax AVX](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/root.c#L694) explicitly probes the **current root** in its PV table. With equal bounds at that search level and [`USE_PREVIOUS_SEARCH` enabled](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/settings.h#L116), it can [walk the saved line](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/root.c#L587) to choose the starting depth/selectivity and seed the score. The [match adapter](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Benchmark/EdaxMatchPlayer.cs#L383) normally sends intervening moves when it can synchronize; it does not always reset the board. This establishes an opportunity, not a measured Edax hit rate.

**A/B in two steps:** (A) On the same board and side, seed **only the legal move**; keep the ordinary depth-one start. (B) On a verified warm hit, start below the previous **remaining** depth and re-search with the normal full root window. Require matching evaluator/search settings, unrestricted root moves and compatible selectivity; on a miss, pass mismatch, new game or changed settings, use the current path. Keep a quick legal fallback, and **never report the cached depth as newly completed**. This changes *where to start on a later turn*, not Phase 4's *how far to step within a turn* or Phase 5's warm-up of a cold solve.

**Deciding measurement:** Replay identical consecutive game positions, with TT retained per engine, and report hit rate, milliseconds spent rebuilding shallower iterations, time to the first fresh completed result, and move quality on PV hits **and** opponent deviations. Include cold controls. Normal [mid-bench clears hash per position](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Benchmark/BenchCommands.cs#L156); it cannot establish this benefit. If shallow iterations are already almost free on hits, keep at most the ordering seed.

### 2. Change the depth of the midgame ProbCut verification, not just its margin

[Stello](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine.cpp#L31) and [Egaroucid](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/multi_probcut.hpp#L74) use approximately half depth, with matching parity. Egaroucid's nearby `+2/-2` alternatives are **commented out**; [Edax's configurable fraction](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/midgame.c#L339) also [defaults to the half-depth schedule](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/options.c#L70) at these depths. So this is a new Stello opportunity, **not** evidence that either opponent wins by using shorter probes. Phase 4 proposes recursion and fitting errors for depth pairs; those do not choose a different verification depth.

I compared alternative probes on the [historical nonselective search values](improve-stello-strength-findings.md#L307) in `Training data/mpc-values.csv`. For each target depth, use the **same** rows with 28–39 discs, exclude rows whose searched values are all zero, and exclude any paired score with magnitude at least 32,000 as in the [trainer](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Trainer/MpcCommand.cs#L307). Entries are RMS `(deep - shallow)` in discs; the middle probe column is today's depth choice, **not** today's evaluator accuracy.

| Target depth | Positions | Two plies cheaper | Usual probe | Two plies deeper |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 3,590 | d2: 4.39 | d4: 3.23 | d6: 2.65 |
| 12 | 3,590 | d4: 3.70 | d6: 2.80 | d8: 2.39 |
| 13 | 3,587 | d5: 3.50 | d7: 2.66 | d9: 2.28 |

**Trial:** On non-PV nodes well beyond the window, test `s-2` with a separately calibrated wider margin; otherwise retain `s`. Keep recursion off initially. Log probe time, accepted cuts and cost of failed verifications. Fit on current-engine positions, audit false cuts against deeper nonselective results on held-out games, and compare at **matched false-cut rate**. These old root scores are not the distribution of live cut nodes; they show why shortening all probes while retaining the current margin is a bad bet.

### 3. Test coarser heuristic scores inside the search

Stello [converts raw weights to hundredths of a disc](../Stello.Cpp/src/eval/eval.hpp#L12); the [built-in model was trained at 16 units/disc](making-stello-5-0-findings.md#L485), so most adjacent attainable scores are already several hundredths apart. Both [Edax AVX](../edax-reversi-AVX-master/src/midgame.c#L140) and [Egaroucid](../Egaroucid-main/src/engine/evaluate_simd.hpp#L558) round their heuristic evaluation to **whole discs**. A tiny improvement that triggers a [full-window re-search in Stello](../Stello.Cpp/src/search/engine_impl.hpp#L607) can be a tie in the other engines. Conversely, rounding can hide the only better move. This is a plausible difference in tree size, not proof of the source of the gap.

**Cheap falsification first:** Count null-window challenges that actually trigger a re-search, their cost and the final improvement in discs. If close challenges are rare or cheap, stop. Otherwise compare current precision with quarter-, then half-disc heuristic leaves; try whole discs only if warranted. Keep the raw evaluator for move ordering, use sign-symmetric rounding, make the leaf shortcut and TT bounds consistent with the rounded score, and leave terminal/WLD/exact scores and external units alone. Start with MPC off to isolate ties; then verify margins with MPC on. Measure root tie rate, full re-search time, lost best moves and disc loss. This changes **search resolution**, not trained weights or Phase 6's training quantization.

## What to run first

1. **Establish a comparable baseline.** Replay identical boards with book and pondering off, one thread first, same wall limits (500 ms and 2 s), actual elapsed time recorded. Split ordinary midgame (e.g. 40+ empties) from transition positions (e.g. 24–32). Separate cold single positions from warm game sequences. Record the last **fully completed** ordinary root depth, attempted depth plus root moves finished/available, selective terminal depth and setting, and proven WLD/exact separately. Mark unavailable opponent fields as unknown rather than converting an observer line into a completed iteration.
2. **Measure hash-first and warm-root move seeding independently** against that baseline. Also run fixed-depth searches: wall time, nodes, ordering evaluations, first-move cutoffs, PVS re-search time and usable TT/MPC cutoffs. At eight threads also measure extra nodes for the same depth versus one thread; don't credit Phase 4 for Phase 3 parallel scaling. Test the TT redesign at equal memory, then stride before aspiration. Leave reductions and score rounding until there is a baseline for missed moves.
3. **Keep the strength gate.** Use [positions with every move scored exactly](../Stello.Net/tools/Stello.Benchmark/Data/midgame-exact.txt) for disc loss near the transition; for earlier boards use longer independent reference searches and label disagreements as such, not exact losses. Only a surviving change goes to the [6.0 paired gate](making-stello-6-0-plan.md#L35): **587 starts / 1,174 games at 500 ms**, lower 95% score bound above 50% for a strength claim, confirmed on 600 games at 2 s. No engine changes or new matches were made for this report.
